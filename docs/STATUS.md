# Status

## Decisions

- **A separate app, on the model of NetsuIcon.** A font is a set of glyphs that must agree, with spacing, kerning and a file format of its own: another trade than an animated icon. The architecture is the same: a pure `core`, small validated ops, a local MCP server, a read-only app that follows the files.
- **Our own JSON document, not UFO.** One `.nfont.json` per font, small enough for an agent to read in parts. Outlines are SVG path data in font units, y up: agents write SVG paths well. A UFO export can come later.
- **OTF with CFF outlines through opentype.js (MIT).** It runs in the browser too, so the app proofs text with the real compiled font. opentype.js writes no kerning: `core/src/gpos.ts` writes it as GPOS (`kern`, one PairPos lookup that skips marks) and `core/src/sfnt.ts` adds a legacy `kern` table for older apps.
- **Components are flattened on export.** CFF has no components in opentype.js; the document keeps them so that accents follow their base letter.
- **Shapes, not only paths.** An agent builds a glyph from rect, ellipse, ring, arc, line and polygon; core turns them into a path, each contour the right way. The document still holds plain paths.
- **Languages are counted.** 62 languages in Latin, Greek, Cyrillic, Armenian, Georgian, Hebrew, Arabic, Devanagari, Thai and Lao script, each with its list of characters; an Arabic language also counts its joining forms and lam-alef, a Devanagari one its half forms, reph, ra below and two conjuncts. `compose` makes accented letters from base and marks, Greek and Cyrillic letters shaped as Latin ones, and Arabic letters from skeleton and dots with all their forms. Khmer, Myanmar and the Indic scripts below are said to be unsupported.
- **Features come from glyph names** (2026-11-06). `uni0628.init` is the initial form of beh, `f_i` a ligature, `a.ss01` a stylistic set, `g.alt` an alternate. `core/src/features.ts` reads the rules once; the export writes them as GSUB through opentype.js, and `proof` applies the same rules. No separate feature file for the agent to keep in step.
- **Marks are placed by anchors computed from the ink** (2026-11-06). A glyph that draws a combining character with advance 0 is a mark; `core/src/marks.ts` gives it an anchor (the bottom of an above mark, the top of a below one) and every letter one (the top and bottom of its ink). `core/src/gpos.ts` writes GDEF and GPOS (mark, mkmk) by hand: opentype.js writes neither. No anchor is drawn by the agent.
- **Devanagari features from names**: `.half`, `uni0930.rphf`, `uni0930.blwf`, conjuncts by their parts (akhn), under the `dev2` script tag. The browser reorders the syllable itself; `core/src/indic.ts` does the same for `proof`.
- **Bengali, Gurmukhi, Gujarati and Tamil** (2026-11-16). `core/src/indic.ts` holds one table for the Indic scripts NetsuFont shapes: their OpenType tags (bng2, gur2, gjr2, tml2), the vowel signs drawn before the consonant, those drawn in two parts (ো ৌ ொ ோ ௌ: text engines split them, `compose` makes their glyph from the parts), the letters ra that make a reph, and where the reph goes (after the vowel signs in Devanagari, before them elsewhere, as HarfBuzz does). Names give the forms in every one of them: `.half`, `.rphf`, `.blwf`, `.pstf` (the Bengali ya-phala), conjuncts by their parts (akhn), a letter and its vowel by their parts (psts: Tamil கு). Languages bn, as, pa, gu, ta count their letters and the forms chosen in `core/src/indicLanguages.ts`: a common set, not every conjunct. Verified with HarfBuzz on a test font of boxes: 19 syllables (split vowels, reph before and after a vowel sign, ya-phala, ra-phala, conjuncts, Tamil கு டி, Gurmukhi ra below, Gujarati reph) give the same glyphs in the same order as `proof`. Not verified: a font drawn in these scripts, a reader, Chrome (it uses HarfBuzz), Windows apps. Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia and Sinhala, whose consonants take forms below in every cluster, are still unsupported.
- **Odia and Malayalam** (2026-11-17). The same table: Odia (ory2) has one vowel sign before its consonant, three in two parts, a reph after its consonant; Malayalam (mlm2) three before, three in two parts, the ra after a virama drawn before its consonant (`.pref`, moved by `proof` and the engine) and the dot reph ൎ drawn after the consonant it is typed before. Verified with HarfBuzz on a test font of boxes: 17 syllables give the same glyphs in the same order as `proof`. Nirmala UI imported as a model gives or 91/100 and ml 115/119 forms by name. Telugu and Kannada came next (below).
- **Telugu and Kannada** (2026-11-17). Nirmala UI draws each consonant with each vowel sign as one glyph (కి కా కు, ಕಿ ಕು): `te` counts 385 of them (11 signs), `kn` 238 (7 signs; ೀ ೇ ೈ ೋ are those and a length mark the engine adds), with each consonant's form below and the conjuncts క్ష జ్ఞ. They are ligatures named by their parts (psts). `compose {syllables:"te"}` makes them for the consonants drawn: `<consonant>.base` (the consonant without its head stroke, or the consonant) and the parts of the sign side by side. In both scripts the engine moves a vowel sign after the base consonant, before its forms below (క్కి is ka-i and ka below), and splits Kannada ೋ in three; the export writes the longest ligature of a feature first, as engines take the first that matches. Kannada has a reph after the syllable; Telugu writes none. Verified with HarfBuzz on a test font of boxes: 31 syllables give the same glyphs in the same order as `proof`. A test font without ೕ made HarfBuzz keep ೀ ೇ ೋ whole: an engine splits a sign only when the font has every part. Not verified: a font drawn in these scripts, a reader, that one shape of base and sign side by side reads well (the model redraws each).
- **Sinhala** (2026-11-22). Its block is not on the common plan: the table now takes a script's own virama (al-lakuna, U+0DCA) and range of consonants. ෙ ෛ are drawn before the consonant; ේ ො ෝ ෞ are split (ෝ in three). As in Telugu, the model draws each consonant with ් ු ූ as one glyph: `si` counts those 123, made by `compose {syllables:"si"}`. HarfBuzz leaves the al-lakuna that comes from a split ේ out of the consonant's ligature; `proof` now marks the parts of a split sign and keeps them out of conjuncts. Verified with HarfBuzz on a test font of boxes: 13 syllables give the same glyphs as `proof`. Not done: conjuncts joined by ZWJ (ක්‍ෂ, the touching letters), the explicit reph. Not verified: a font drawn in Sinhala.
- **Khmer** (2026-11-22). The same table, with the coeng (U+17D2) as virama: every consonant has a form under another (`.blwf`), ro under a coeng is drawn before the consonant (`uni179A.pref`, as Malayalam ra), េ ែ ៃ are drawn before, and ើ ឿ ៀ ោ ៅ are split by the engine into េ and their own code, which the font draws as the part after the letter (so `compose` does not make them). `km` counts the letters, signs, Khmer digits, 33 forms below and the ro. Verified with HarfBuzz (its own Khmer shaper) on a test font of boxes: 12 syllables, ស្ត្រី included, give the same glyphs in the same order as `proof`. Measured in Leelawadee UI, the Windows font that draws Khmer. Not verified: a font drawn in Khmer, the ligatures Leelawadee adds (ka and aa joined).
- **Myanmar** (2026-11-22). The same table: the virama U+1039 stacks a consonant under another (`.blwf`); ေ and the medial ra ြ are drawn before the consonant, in that order (the table now moves every such sign, in the order a script lists them); the kinzi (nga, asat, virama before a consonant) is a reph of three letters, `uni1004.rphf`, drawn after the consonant (a script now says what follows its ra to make the reph). `my` counts 33 stacked forms and the kinzi. Verified with HarfBuzz (its Myanmar shaper) on a test font of boxes: 15 syllables, kinzi and medials included, give the same glyphs in the same order as `proof`. Measured in Myanmar Text. Not done: the medial ra in several widths, as Myanmar Text draws it. Not verified: a font drawn in Myanmar.
- **Tibetan** (2026-11-22). Its letters written under another have codes of their own (U+0F90 on) and are combining marks: drawn as marks of advance 0 below the baseline, the GPOS (mark and mkmk, a `tibt` script record) stacks them under the letter and the vowel under them; nothing to reorder. `bo` counts 30 letters, 36 letters below, the vowels, tsheg, shad and digits. Verified with HarfBuzz (its universal shaper) on a test font of boxes: in སྐྱུ the ka, ya and u below stack at the same places as in `proof`. Measured in Microsoft Himalaya. Not done: the short ra over a letter below it (རྐ); the full ra is used. Not verified: a font drawn in Tibetan.
- **A model font for a script the agent does not know** (2026-11-06). The blind Hindi run showed that an agent draws Devanagari from memory badly. `import_font` reads .ttc collections (Nirmala UI on Windows), and `remove_font` takes the model out again (to `fonts/.trash`, git-ignored). The skill says to look at the model and never copy its paths.
- **An imported font keeps its forms by name** (2026-11-08). `core/src/featureNames.ts` reads the GSUB of the file (extension lookups included) and renames the glyphs it makes to the names features are read from: `uni0628.init`, `uni0915.half`, `uni0930.rphf`, `uni0915_uni094D_uni0937`; the letters first get their usual names. Verified on Nirmala UI: half forms, reph, क्ष and ज्ञ found; its ra below was not. `look_glyphs {glyphs, model}` shows a model's conjunct even before the agent draws one.
- **The accents of the Bible** (2026-11-08). `hbo` counts the letters, the points, the te'amim U+0591–U+05AF, maqaf, paseq and sof pasuq. Most accents sit above or below their letter; pashta, zarqa and telisha qetana go past its left edge, telisha gedola past its right, yetiv and dehi below and right (`aboveLeft`, `aboveRight`, `belowRight` in `core/src/marks.ts`). Drawn on a test font: `proof` sets Genesis 1:1 with its points and accents. In Chrome the side accents fall back to centred (above).
- **Japanese from strokes** (2026-11-10). Kanji are too many to draw one by one: `draw_kanji` reads KanjiVG (the centre line of every stroke of 6,703 characters) and draws each stroke with a pen of the font (width, ends, contrast along an angle), one outline per stroke (`core/src/kanji.ts`). KanjiVG is CC BY-SA 3.0: it is downloaded by `pnpm --filter @netsufont/server kanjivg` into `~/.netsufont/kanjivg`, never into the repository, and a font made with it is CC BY-SA. The lists of kanji come from Unihan (kJoyoKanji, kJinmeiyoKanji; Unicode License). `ja` counts the kana, the punctuation and the 2,136 jōyō (2,140 code points). Verified: the 2,140 jōyō and 176 kana drawn in one call each (7 s), a font file of 3.7 MB and an OTF of 1.7 MB; `proof` and Chrome set Japanese text with it. A blind run (Kumo, 2026-11-10) made ja and en complete in 35 calls; its findings are in the skill. Not verified: vertical writing, Windows apps. The app is slow with a font this size: it compiles the whole OTF at every change.
- **Chinese and Korean from strokes** (2026-11-16). Chinese: `draw_kanji` also reads Make Me a Hanzi (the median of each stroke of 9,574 characters, from the Arphic fonts: Arphic Public License), downloaded by `pnpm --filter @netsufont/server hanzi` into `~/.netsufont/hanzi`, never committed. The medians are smoothed (Catmull-Rom), their corners kept sharp past 50°. `zh-Hans` counts the 3,500 characters of level 1 of the 2013 table (Unihan kTGH); `zh-Hant` their traditional forms (kTraditionalVariant, the Big5 one first), 3,498. Make Me a Hanzi has all of level 1; five traditional forms come from KanjiVG and 叄 has no data. Korean: the 51 letters are centre lines written for NetsuFont (`core/src/hangul.ts`); a syllable places its initial, its vowel and its final by the shape of the vowel, and the pen keeps its width however small the letter. `ko` counts the 2,350 syllables of KS X 1001 (read from EUC-KR), the letters and the Latin punctuation. Verified: the 3,500 simplified and 3,497 traditional drawn in 13 s each (a font file of 9 MB for both), the 2,350 syllables in 5 s (2 MB); `proof` and Chrome set Chinese and Korean text with them. A blind run (Yunshan, 2026-11-16) made zh-Hans, zh-Hant and en complete in 31 calls; its hanzi read as a fine brush (kai), not as Song. Since then `terminals: "song"` makes strokes level and upright and adds the triangles of Song faces, and `.zht` glyphs give traditional Chinese its centred punctuation (locl, languages ZHT and ZHH). Not verified: a Korean or Chinese reader, Korean by an agent with only the skill; the app was slow with such a font; see below. The app's text view marks its text with a language (picked with a sample, or alone): verified in Chrome on 2026-11-17 with a test font, the full-width comma moves to the centre when the text is zh-Hant and stays low otherwise.
- **Second CJK blind runs** (2026-11-16). Hanbit (Korean with Latin): ko and en complete in 28 calls, comfortable at 48 and 20 px; Hangul looked light beside the Latin and syllables without a final small: their boxes are larger now, ㅇ ㅎ smaller, and the skill says to match the colour of the two in a mixed line. Mingshu (Song Chinese with Latin): complete in about 72 calls, but still read as a brush hand: `terminals: "song"` now also tapers the falling strokes (撇 to a point, 捺 wider at its foot). The data is a brush's: the result is a Song-flavoured face, and the skill says so. `proof` takes `lang` (zh-Hant) and uses the local forms.
- **The app asks for one font at a time** (2026-11-16). `/api/fonts` lists the fonts in short (1 KB) and `/api/fonts/<name>` gives one, read again only when its file changed (the store keeps fonts parsed by their time of writing). The OTF of the text view is compiled in a Web Worker once the font is still for 300 ms, and the grid draws 240 cells, then more. Measured: the list in 6 ms, a font of 4,760 glyphs (5.4 MB) in 121 ms, its OTF compiled without freezing the page.
- **Kerning in GPOS, and WOFF2** (2026-11-16). Today's engines read kerning from GPOS; a `kern` table alone is ignored by some apps. The pairs are a PairPos lookup (format 1, the change of the first glyph's advance) under the feature `kern`, for every script, with the flag that skips marks. `export_font` also writes a WOFF2: the OTF's tables in one Brotli stream (`core/src/woff2.ts`; Brotli comes from Node in the server, core stays free of it). Verified: HarfBuzz (uharfbuzz) applies the pairs of Galet (AV −60, LT −70) and none when `kern` is off; it kerns a Hebrew pair across a qamats; fontTools reads Rivet's WOFF2 back with every table equal to the OTF's (24 KB to 8.8 KB); Chrome loads that WOFF2 and its line is 597 px with kerning, 618 px without. Not verified: Word, InDesign, macOS.
- **Scaled components** (2026-11-22). A component takes `scale` (more than 0, at most 4), applied from its origin when the outline is flattened, so a form below or a conjunct can use a smaller letter; three blind runs had made scaled copies by hand. Its strokes get thinner with it.
- **A lint of the style** (2026-11-22). `lint {name}` measures each letter's stroke on lines through its body (curves flattened, the nonzero rule, so overlaps count once): at 35, 50 and 65 % of its height each run of ink is measured across and up through its middle, the shorter kept; the median of a height, then the thinnest height. A letter whose stroke is 1.5 times heavier or lighter than the median of its script (five letters at least) is named; so is a round letter with curves (o c e s O C G S Q) that does not pass the baseline and its x-height or cap height. Tried on the fonts of the repository: Rivet, Galet and Netsu Pixel give nothing; Fulgur gives ж (364 against about 130). Thresholds chosen by these tries: at 1.35 the diagonals of z and K came out, and taking every height brought back the crossings of x and ж. Since then it also names a Latin letter whose side bearing differs from that of n, o, H or O on a side of the same shape by more than a quarter and 15 units (j and the tail of Q left out, a monospaced font skipped); none in Rivet, Galet, Fulgur or Netsu Pixel. Not measured: widths, contrast; a font drawn in scripts other than Latin, Greek and Cyrillic.
- **The styles of a family** (2026-11-22). A style is one document. `create_font {from}` copies a font's glyphs, metrics and kerning under a new id and style, to be redrawn. The export reads the style name (`core/src/style.ts`): its weight (Thin 100 to Black 900) in `usWeightClass`, and the names and bits apps group styles by. Regular, Bold, Italic and Bold Italic share one family name (name ID 1) with the bold and italic bits set; any other weight is a family of its own for Windows ("Fulgur Light", style Regular or Italic), with the family and style in name IDs 16 and 17. Before, every style was weight 500 with the Regular bit, so Word could not link a Bold. Verified with fontTools: Fulgur exported as Bold is 700, fsSelection 32, macStyle 1, names Fulgur / Bold; as Light Italic, 300, italic, Fulgur Light / Italic, 16 and 17 Fulgur / Light Italic. Not verified: Word's B button with two installed styles, macOS. Not done: weights drawn from each other (interpolating masters, emboldening outlines); each style is drawn.
- **TrueType export** (2026-11-22). `export_font` also writes a .ttf: the tables of the OTF, with `glyf`, `loca` and `maxp` 1 in place of `CFF `, each cubic curve split into quadratics until they stray less than half a unit, and `hmtx` written again with each left side bearing equal to the glyph's xMin (TrueType engines place an outline by it; opentype.js writes 0 for a CFF font, and a first try shifted every glyph left). No hinting, `post` version 3 (no glyph names). Verified: fontTools reads Rivet, Galet and Netsu Pixel back, every glyph's bounds within 0.5 unit of the OTF's and its area within 0.7 %; HarfBuzz gives the same advances; Chrome sets Rivet's TTF and OTF at the same width with the same kerning. Not verified: Windows installation, Word, unhinted rendering at small sizes on Windows.
- **GPOS kerning read on import** (2026-11-17). opentype.js reads neither GPOS Extension lookups nor most PairPos: `core/src/gposRead.ts` reads the lookups of the feature `kern` (PairPos formats 1 and 2, inside Extensions), the first rule for a pair winning, the pairs of classes spread to their glyphs (class 0 left out), at most 50,000 pairs. A `kern` table adds the pairs GPOS lacks. Measured: Segoe UI gives 22,804 pairs (A V −140, T o −200), Arial 4,386, Bahnschrift is cut at 50,000.
- **Our own shaping for `proof`.** `core/src/shape.ts` does a simple bidi (levels, no explicit embeddings), Arabic joining, ligatures and the optional features; marks sit on the anchors the GPOS has. The app's text view is set by the browser with the real OTF.
- **The guide holds the format, the skill the method.** `server/src/guide.ts` is what every agent pays for; `skills/netsu-type-design` is loaded when a font is drawn.
- **No GPL tool.** FontForge and Fontra are GPL; fontTools (MIT) may later be called from the server for TTF, WOFF2 and GPOS.

## Verified

- `pnpm check` and `pnpm test` pass: 91 tests in core, 5 on the MCP tools.
- Every recipe in `skills/netsu-type-design/references/construction.md` was drawn and set in `proof` (2026-10-25).
- A blind run: a Sonnet agent with only the skill drew Galet (fr, pl complete; a–z, A–Z, figures, punctuation, 76 kerning pairs) and exported it, in about 95 calls. A second run on the corrected skill drew Rivet (en, ru, el complete, 230 glyphs, 90 kerning pairs) in about 61 calls. The findings of both runs were fixed; the text corrected after the second has not run blind. Details in NetsuSkills `docs/JOURNAL.md`.
- Netsu Pixel covers en, fr, es, de, nl, its accents placed by `compose`.
- An exported OTF reads back with its family, metrics, advances, outlines and kerning (test in `core/src/core.test.ts`).
- The example font was drawn through the tools (`seed`), exported (12 112 bytes), and set as text in the app by Chrome from the compiled OTF.

- Arabic, verified 2026-11-06 on a monoline test font drawn through the tools (35 skeleton glyphs, 9 letters composed with their forms): Chrome sets the exported OTF right to left with its joining forms, lam-alef and `ss01`; `proof` sets the same words in the same order. Not verified: Windows apps, Word, Persian and Urdu text, lam-alef with a harakat between (the ligature skips marks now; not tried in a browser).

- **Marks and the Hebrew shaper** (found and fixed 2026-11-08). HarfBuzz (Chrome, Android) reads GPOS for Hebrew only under the `hebr` script; our GPOS had DFLT alone, so HarfBuzz set the marks by its own fallback, and the earlier pictures that matched `proof` matched only because the fallback also centres marks. `gpos.ts` now writes a script record for DFLT and every script the font draws (`scriptTags`). Verified with uharfbuzz on the Hebrew test font (`zz-hebrew`): with the GPOS the points, dagesh, shin dot, holam, pashta (past the left edge) and the accents below (stacked under the point) move as the anchors say, and moving an anchor moves the mark; without it they fall back to the centre. No mark touches another in Bereshit 1:1. Not tried: Chrome after the fix, Windows apps. The other scripts' shapers read DFLT already; they get their own record too. Verified in Chrome on 2026-11-10: a mark of the frame kind stays below and right where drawn, telisha gedola goes past the right of its letter, pashta past the left of its alef.
- **GPOS lookups are Extensions** (2026-11-10). A mark that attaches to every glyph of a Japanese font (2,400 bases) made a lookup past the 64 KiB a 16-bit offset reaches, and the export failed. Each lookup is now an Extension (type 9) whose 32-bit offsets point at subtables written after the LookupList. Verified with fontTools on a font of 2,414 glyphs: 7 mark-to-base subtables and the mark-to-mark one read back.
